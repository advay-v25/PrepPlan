# Roadmap - Team presentation notes

Companion to **Roadmap: Product and Workflow Guide**. This describes the proposed product, not a statement that all features are live. No new application functionality was built to produce the presentation.

## The 30-second explanation

Roadmap is a competitive-exam study planner, starting with CFA Level I. It helps students decide what to study next, how to approach it, where it fits around real life and what to revisit after practice. Students keep using their own courses and materials. Roadmap connects their learning to a realistic weekly plan, checks understanding through focused diagnostics and helps adjust the following week. It guides; it does not replace a course or promise a pass.

## Suggested presentation sequence

Allow roughly 12-15 minutes for the guide, then discussion. The time is a presentation suggestion, not a product study-hour recommendation.

### 1. The idea

Lead with the problem: having access to content does not automatically tell a student what to do next. The three responsibilities are study with a method, fit study around life and build confidence through evidence.

### 2. Who it serves

Explain coaching, self-study, returning and variable-schedule journeys. These can overlap: a working student may attend coaching and also be returning after a break. We adapt to their context, not a fixed personality label.

The commercial concept is paid planning and accountability, not selling someone else's course material. Pricing and packaging are outside this presentation.

### 3. The weekly loop

Walk through the six boxes: understand the week, suggest work, review and accept, study and record, check understanding, reflect and adapt. Emphasise the return arrow. A first-week timetable alone is not the product.

### 4. First-time setup

The questions have jobs: identify the learning priority, locate the source, understand real constraints and prepare useful practice. Unknown exam timing or unavailable files do not block support. Explain that a draft is proposed work until the student accepts it.

### 5. The pages

Home answers what matters now. My Week organises it. Curriculum shows structure and progress. Practice provides learning evidence. Coach connects and helps operate these experiences. Sources live inside relevant flows with a central manager in Settings, not a Materials main tab.

### 6. A real study task

Use the illustrative task to distinguish a study method from a lesson. The app explains the goal, source, approach and follow-up. The student learns the substantive material from their own source. A suggested method is optional guidance, not another checklist required to count their effort.

### 7. Practice and confidence

A diagnostic is a focused test for finding understanding and gaps. It is not an entrance exam. Separate studying a section, demonstrating a skill and feeling confident. Specific evidence supports confidence more honestly than a decorative readiness percentage.

### 8. Life balance

Explain the difference between less work, shorter sittings and moving work. They are not interchangeable. The app protects confirmed commitments and shows trade-offs. A student can accept flexible tasks without assigning every one a time.

### 9. Sources and restricted portals

Explain that Roadmap needs a verified curriculum and assessment foundation of its own, but not a copy of every student's course. A student can link a portal lesson and confirm what it covers. Roadmap must not pretend to have read inaccessible content. Permitted imports can improve detail later; they are not essential to the basic journey.

### 10. The Coach

The Coach is more than a chat window: it can propose a week, start supported diagnostics, explain progress and direct the student to a specific learning outcome or source. Meaningful calendar changes need a concrete preview and confirmation. It does not provide whole-subject lessons or quietly change plans.

### 11. The example week

Read the example as a story rather than a feature checklist. The student attends class, consolidates, checks understanding, encounters a disruption and adjusts. The next week uses what actually happened instead of simply copying the previous schedule.

### 12. Delivery and proof

This is the intended workflow, not a live-feature checklist. Foundation verification, connected planning, practice evidence and the second-week loop must be demonstrated before calling the product useful. A limited pilot must be labelled; full CFA coverage must be verified before it is advertised.

### 13. Discussion

Ask the team whether the next action is clear, both student types are supported, the teaching boundary is understandable, the student retains control and claims about sources/evidence are honest.

## Likely questions and short answers

**Is this another CFA course?** No. It helps organise and verify learning from the student's sources, with specific feedback and source direction rather than full-course teaching.

**Does the app decide how many hours everyone must study?** No. It proposes scoped work around confirmed constraints and lets the student approve the trade-offs. Suggested durations are estimates, not a universal quota.

**What if the student misses a week?** Keep the history, ask what changed and propose a manageable restart. Do not assume every unreported activity was missed or automatically double future work.

**What if the student has not chosen an exam date?** They can still organise the next seven days. The app does not invent an appointment or countdown.

**What if a portal cannot be downloaded?** Use a student-confirmed lesson reference and curriculum mapping. Supported practice can use Roadmap's own authorised assessment foundation, with honest limitations about personal-source access.

**How does confidence improve?** That is a product aim, not a guarantee. The experience makes specific learning progress visible, gives manageable practice choices and helps the student recover from disruption. Felt confidence and demonstrated understanding remain separate.

**What do we mean by an LOS?** A Learning Outcome Statement: a specific skill or understanding expected in the curriculum. We use it to connect study, practice and source guidance.

**Can the Coach edit the plan?** It can propose and apply confirmed changes through the same plan used by the pages. It cannot invent successful actions or bypass student confirmation for consequential changes.

**How will we know the prototype works?** Demonstrate a student going from context to accepted work to diagnostic evidence to a revised or second week, including a real-life disruption and a no-upload source path.

## Closing sentence

Roadmap does not ask students to organise their lives around a perfect theoretical plan. It helps them make useful learning decisions within the week they actually have.
