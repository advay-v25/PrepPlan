# Roadmap — competitive landscape and India market strategy

**Prepared:** 20 September 2026  
**Status:** Strategy working document; not a revenue forecast or legal opinion  
**Launch scope:** CFA Level I, 2027 curriculum onward; India first

## Executive conclusion

Roadmap should **not** sell itself as a CFA course, AI tutor, PDF study app, or simply a larger question bank. CFA Institute and established prep providers already supply trusted curriculum, instruction, practice, mocks, planning, and analytics. Several newer products also advertise AI plans and SWOT analysis. Roadmap's potential opening is a **provider-neutral preparation control system**: it turns classes and self-study into a calendar, records what actually happened, tests only eligible completed work, distinguishes performance from confidence and activity, and proposes a credible next week after deviations.

That is a product hypothesis, **not a claim of proven superiority**. The first commercial proof must be that candidates repeatedly complete the loop **plan → do → test → interpret → adapt**, find the resulting week useful, and will pay for it in addition to their existing resources. Roadmap complements, rather than replaces, Zell, FinTree, Mark Meldrum, Kaplan, CFA Institute, and other teachers or materials.

### Evidence conventions

- **Verified market data** means published by CFA Institute. An *exam administration* is not necessarily a unique candidate, and an all-level total is not a Level I total.
- **Provider claim** means a company's own description or number. Counts may be cumulative, global, and across many courses; they are not audited active Indian CFA Level I enrolments.
- **Roadmap design** means founder-approved direction, not necessarily a shipped, reliable feature. Current implementation must be audited separately before making public claims.
- **Illustrative assumption** means a scenario for planning or validation, not an observed market fact.
- Public product pages establish what competitors *advertise*, not how well those features work in practice.

## 1. Indian market: size, opportunity, and limits

CFA Institute's FY2025 annual report shows approximately **40,500 CFA Program exam administrations in India**, up from 32,000 in FY2024, across all levels. CFA Institute separately estimated approximately **50,000 Indian candidates taking CFA Program examinations in 2026**, and said new Level I candidate numbers had grown about 20% year-on-year over the prior three years. These facts indicate a meaningful and growing India market; they do **not** identify the number of unique annual Indian Level I buyers or guarantee continuing growth. [CFA Institute FY2025 annual report](https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/docs/about/governance/cfa_annual-report-2025_final_02-27_w-financials.pdf); [CFA Institute India overview](https://www.cfainstitute.org/about/expansion-india-investment-industry).

For preliminary modelling only, use **25,000–35,000 annual Indian Level I candidates** as a *working range*, and replace it with verified level-specific data before using it in investor material. Do not multiply the all-level 50,000 by an assumed annual 20% growth rate. Do not count all finance students, all three CFA levels, or coaching providers' lifetime alumni as Roadmap's addressable Level I market.

This is a focused niche with four exam-window cohorts, rather than a uniformly active year-round consumer market. A candidate may naturally leave after sitting the paper. Acquisition, product usage, support load, and revenue should therefore be measured by **exam cohort and attempt**, not only by monthly active users.

## 3. Feature-by-feature gap and opportunity

| Capability | Competitive reality / where Roadmap may lag | Roadmap's plausible advantage and required improvement |
|---|---|---|
| **First plan and onboarding** | UWorld and 300Hours already generate exam-date plans with little setup. Long forms risk abandonment. [UWorld](https://finance.uworld.com/cfa/level-1/question-bank/); [300Hours](https://300hours.com/cfa-study-planner/) | Capture exam window/date, class pattern, unavailable time, current position and sitting constraints only when they visibly affect the first week. Week one is a calibration week, not a claim to know true capacity. |
| **Long-range roadmap** | Prep providers already sequence curricula and mocks. A generic month-by-month path is not a moat. | Distinguish long-range direction from a specific accepted week. Preserve final revision/mock time, show pressure and choices when a candidate accelerates, slows, defers or changes exam date. |
| **Calendar** | General calendar products are far more mature in timeboxing, conflict detection and integrations. | Deliver credible day/week/agenda views, fixed versus movable blocks, protected commitments, floating work, class changes, clear conflict previews, and explicit acceptance of proposed replans. Mobile usability is essential. |
| **Class-going candidates** | Institutes know their own schedules and may provide an activity plan. | Work with *any* institute. A moved/cancelled/one-off class and its actual coverage should update the proposed study week and paper scope without rewriting historical weeks. |
| **Learning capture** | Any to-do app can mark a task done. Manual mapping can become onerous. | Record attendance, study, practice and module/LOS coverage in a few taps from Home, Calendar or Curriculum. Treat uncertain mappings and missing logs as unknown, never silently as complete or missed. |
| **Weekly tests** | CFA Institute owns the official practice benchmark; established banks have thousands of items. A promised 20,000-item bank is an editorial liability, not automatically an advantage. | Assemble a quality-controlled, unseen paper from **eligible work actually marked done**. Distinguish diagnostic (new material), revisionary (revisited material) and mixed papers. Respect the 90-question ceiling and honest bank-insufficiency fallback. |
| **Results and SWOT** | Preparoo already presents SWOT; Kaplan, UWorld and others show strengths and weaknesses. [Preparoo](https://fin.preparoo.app/); [Kaplan](https://www.schweser.com/cfa/level-1/study-packages) | Make analysis defensible: accuracy, timing, mandatory confidence, breadth, sample size, difficulty, freshness, repeated misses and untested scope. Label correlated “opportunities/threats” as hypotheses rather than conclusions from a single item. |
| **Weakness repair** | Competitors recommend revision; Kaplan advertises remediation recommendations. | Complete the operational loop: miss → specific repair proposal → conflict-safe calendar placement → re-check → evidence update. Deduplicate recurring repair threads and do not silently modify an accepted week. |
| **Preparedness** | Many products advertise readiness scores, benchmarks or predictions. | Show four separate signals—curriculum position, demonstrated understanding, plan execution and confidence calibration—with uncertainty and sample size. Avoid an opaque percentage or unvalidated pass probability. |
| **Coach** | Teaching providers can answer curriculum questions with faculty or tutors. A non-teaching Coach risks sounding vague. | Make every answer anchored to current evidence, affected topic/module/LOS, decision, expected calendar consequence and uncertainty. Point the user back to approved learning locations; do not teach whole chapters or invent citations. Calendar changes require preview and confirmation. |
| **Mobile and continuity** | Established providers offer mature mobile experiences and some offline access. | Prioritise fast mobile logging, calendar, test continuation and result-to-next-week flow. Consider offline support after real usage demonstrates demand. |
| **Provider independence** | Content-bundled products own both the resource and progress data. Roadmap lacks direct access to their portals. | Keep core planning independent of uploads and portal scraping. Later, support permission-based calendar sync and manual external mock-result capture; do not presume rights to import protected provider content. |

The central competitive test is **not whether Roadmap has more feature names**. It is whether a candidate can say: “My class moved, I only finished two readings, the paper exposed one weak concept, and Roadmap gave me a sensible next week that I accepted.”

## 4. Positioning and messaging

### Primary position

> **Roadmap works alongside your CFA materials and classes. It turns what you actually covered into a realistic week, checks what you can demonstrate, and adjusts the next week when life or learning changes.**

### What Roadmap is

- A CFA-specific execution, testing and accountability layer.
- A calendar that connects commitments, classes, new learning, revision, papers and repairs.
- A transparent evidence system that distinguishes activity and self-confidence from demonstrated understanding.
- A bounded Coach for planning decisions, weak-point direction and proposed rescheduling.

### What Roadmap is not

- A replacement for CFA Institute curriculum, coaching, a teacher or a tutor.
- A claim that AI-generated questions are official CFA Institute questions.
- A material-upload or full-subject-teaching app at launch.
- A pass guarantee, suitability verdict or opaque readiness percentage.

### Avoidable marketing claims

Do not lead with “AI-powered study planner”, “SWOT analysis”, “20,000 questions”, “trained on Schweser”, or “predicts your pass probability”. Competitors already use similar language, and several of these claims create trust, accuracy or content-rights risks. A stronger demonstration is a **before-and-after week** showing exactly why a class, missed block or diagnostic result changed the proposed plan.

## 5. Product and commercial gaps to close before launch

1. **Question quality and rights.** Establish a versioned 2027 curriculum map, original-item authoring, numerical and editorial verification, human review, difficulty/coverage controls, challenge handling and content provenance. The 2027 Level I update particularly affects Quantitative Methods and Equity Investments, so yearly versioning is necessary. Third-party study material requires rights review; model “knowledge” does not eliminate copyright obligations. [CFA Institute 2027 curriculum update](https://www.cfainstitute.org/about/press-room/2026/cfa-institute-announces-updates-to-cfa-program-curriculum)
2. **A trustworthy calendar.** The internal calendar must work even before external sync: real time placement, protected commitments, class logging, conflict warnings, mobile views, floating work, history and non-destructive proposals.
3. **Low-friction input.** Provider-neutrality fails if the student must type extensive details after every class. Quick scope selection, defaults from scheduled blocks, easy correction and explicit uncertainty are essential.
4. **A completed weekly loop.** A test result is not the endpoint. Require logs to be completed or marked unknown, show the week’s factual record, preview repairs/carry-forward/roadmap pressure, and let the candidate accept the next draft.
5. **Coach reliability.** Use controlled tools, response schemas, owned examples and evaluation rubrics. Prompt engineering alone is not a defensible “trained AI model”. Record proposed versus applied changes.
6. **Commercial basics.** Validate exam-window pass versus monthly pricing with actual candidates. Provide Indian payment methods, straightforward trial and access terms, cancellation/refund policy, support and privacy disclosures. Do not copy coaching-provider prices: Roadmap is a complementary purchase.
7. **Evidence of value.** Measure weekly closure, useful accepted changes and paid renewal/word of mouth. Sign-ups and generated plans alone do not validate this product.

**Priority discipline:** Do not add PDF uploads, chapter teaching, flashcards, job placement or a social feed to answer competitors’ breadth. All distract from the weekly decision loop and increase content, operational and legal load.

## 6. Go-to-market in India

### Channel A — direct to candidate

Recruit both self-study and class-going candidates across different exam windows, employment statuses and cities. A trial must carry the user through **at least one paper and the adapted next week**; a trial ending after onboarding would hide the product's value. Test a per-attempt/exam-window price alongside a monthly alternative. Candidate interviews must explicitly ask why the person would pay **on top of** existing CFA Institute access and any coaching purchase.

### Channel B — coaching partnerships

Pilot with two or three smaller or mid-sized institutes before attempting national names. Pitch the institute on student execution *between* classes: class-preparation status, consented aggregate coverage and where students collectively need follow-up. Do not offer to replace faculty explanations, scrape their portal, or send private student calendar details to instructors. A first 50–150-student pilot per institute is enough to learn whether the staff and candidates actually use the workflow.

The candidate account and history must remain useful if the institute partnership ends. Otherwise Roadmap loses its provider-neutral positioning. Institute dashboards should be built only after permissions, privacy boundaries and a direct pilot establish demand.

### Pricing and success scenarios

There is no credible public basis for assigning Roadmap a probability of success. The following numbers are **conditional planning examples**, not forecasts. Assume an unvalidated average realised price of **₹3,999 per Level I attempt**, before taxes, payment fees, marketing, AI, item review, support and partner shares:

| First full exam-year scenario | Paying candidates | Illustrative gross receipts | Reading |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Weak traction | 100 | ~₹4 lakh | Learn from the cohort; not yet a proven business. |
| Credible early traction | 300 | ~₹12 lakh | Some willingness to pay; retention and acquisition economics still unproven. |
| Strong focused traction | 1,000 | ~₹40 lakh | Ambitious for a new standalone product; requires excellent execution or meaningful distribution. |

At the illustrative 25,000–35,000 Level I market range, 1,000 paying candidates would imply roughly 3–4% annual penetration. Do not treat that as a first-year baseline. Partnerships and direct sales should be reported separately, with overlap removed.

### Initial validation scorecard

The following are **proposed go/no-go thresholds**, not industry benchmarks:

- At least **60%** of verified sign-ups activate a first week.
- At least **40%** of activated users complete a first eligible weekly closure.
- At least **25%** complete four closures.
- A majority of interviewed completers can identify a useful schedule change Roadmap made that they would not otherwise have made.
- Paid conversion, acquisition cost, content/support cost per active candidate, and cohort-level renewal or referral are measured—not assumed.
- One institute pilot can run through a full exam cohort without bespoke manual operation by the Roadmap team.

If these fail, fix the input friction, test quality or week-to-week usefulness before adding more AI features.

## 7. Five-year relevance path

| Year | Focus | Expansion condition |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Prove Level I** | Reliable first week, real calendar, rapid class/study logging, reviewed diagnostics, evidence-based repairs, Coach proposals and weekly closure. Direct and small-institute pilots. | Demonstrated repeated use and willingness to pay. |
| **2 — Make Level I dependable at scale** | Broader audited item coverage, mobile refinement, permission-based external calendar sync, external mock-result capture without copying questions, partner cohort controls with explicit consent. | Low item-error rates, positive cohort economics, partner renewal. |
| **3 — Extend within CFA** | Level II-specific curriculum and item-set assessment, return-candidate history, deferral/reattempt routes, mature institute administration. | Level I workflow is stable; Level II editorial and assessment capability is independently validated. |
| **4 — Add one adjacent professional exam** | FRM is a plausible candidate, but it needs its own licensed/verified curriculum map, bank, exam-cycle rules and evaluation—not a CFA prompt with renamed topics. | Documented candidate demand and ability to maintain another exam accurately. |
| **5 — Multi-exam operating system** | Candidate-controlled cross-exam planning, institution integrations where permitted, longitudinal accountability insights and optional human accountability partnerships. | Each exam and partner channel earns expansion through retention and economics. Do not assume CA or JEE merely because the calendar can display their syllabus. |

For India, a Hindi or regional-language **interface and planning explanation** could be valuable later. Assessed CFA terminology and item wording should not be translated casually; fidelity to exam requirements must prevail.

## 8. Research gaps and next fieldwork

1. Obtain an authoritative **India Level I new-to-level candidate count by exam window** before presenting a TAM to investors. CFA Institute's prep-provider materials indicate that city/level candidate intelligence may be available to eligible providers; availability and terms should be checked directly. [CFA Institute prep-provider agreement](https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/docs/programs/cfa-program/prep-provider-agreement_2026_final.pdf)
2. Interview 30–40 Indian Level I candidates: self-study, weekday classes, weekend classes, full-time work, undergraduate study, early and compressed preparation. Observe their *actual* current calendars and tools.
3. Interview 8–12 coaching operators/faculty about between-class follow-through, privacy concerns, willingness to pilot, and whether they would pay or bundle Roadmap.
4. Test willingness to pay with a real first-paper-to-next-week experience. Survey enthusiasm for “AI planning” is not a substitute for paid conversion.
5. Mystery-shop competing apps with consistent tasks: create a plan, move a class, record partial coverage, miss a study block, take a test, and inspect whether the next week changes correctly. Public pages cannot verify execution quality.
6. Review 2027 content rights, CFA marks/non-affiliation language, privacy, student consent and Indian payment/consumer obligations with qualified counsel before accepting real candidates.

## Decision

The recommended wedge is **“the non-panic weekly control system for CFA Level I candidates using any learning provider.”** Build and validate one complete weekly loop before expanding content volume or exam breadth. The strongest eventual asset is not a generic AI model; it is a trustworthy, versioned link between curriculum scope, real-world execution, assessment evidence and calendar decisions—supported by measured candidate outcomes and permission-based distribution partnerships.
