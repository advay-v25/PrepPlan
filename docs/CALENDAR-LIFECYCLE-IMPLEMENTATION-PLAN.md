# Calendar lifecycle: audit and implementation plan

**6 November 2026 · Roadmap development checkout.** This is a test and implementation handoff. The proposed changes below have not been built. It extends the Calendar work in [Home and Calendar closeout](HOME-CALENDAR-CLOSEOUT-IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md). The earlier redistribution gap is partly addressed by migration 20261127; its focused test suite passes. Do not redo that migration.

The goal: **tell the student how and when to work next, within time they actually have**. The accepted week is the record; future weeks are forecasts until confirmed. A student's reported coverage and external mock results guide planning without becoming scored evidence. A missed task is a scheduling fact, not an incorrect answer.

## What's already useful

- Midway entry is not a restart. The ordinary-student onboarding journey passed 46 checks. In the browser-backed fixture (three topics covered, uncertainty marked), the accepted partial week contained **7 study sittings and 3 revision sittings**. The revision drawer gave an activity-based reason, method and definition of done. Its reported uncertainty did not become evidence.
- The four-cycle revision exercise passed **59/59**. It covered targeted maintenance during first pass, revision-only entry after first pass, recorded/partly confirmed/skipped work, carry-forward, rotation and spacing, repair exclusion, staleness, concurrent acceptance and scoped assessment.
- Current-week acceptance consistency passed **18/18**. Timed redistribution passed **16/16**, including short gaps, commitments, recurrence exceptions, midnight and sitting caps. Keep these paths and tests.
- The planner is **deterministic**. Roadmap, the week generator, continuation placement and capacity decide the work. Coach uses deterministic links/proposals; the provider only rewrites the answer for open questions. **A good reply is not proof a plan is correct; a provider outage must not prevent planning.**

## Phase 1 — date, cycle and time integrity

1. **Make the displayed period authoritative.** Resolve one owned active, closed or future period for the requested real date; distinguish these states in the view. A closed week reads its accepted tasks, recorded learning and assessment receipt in the cycle’s timezone; retain continuation links. If only current commitment data is available for old weeks, label it as reconstructed rather than inventing a historic snapshot. A future week shows confirmed commitments and mocks, plus any explicitly labelled forecast. A floating task is scoped to its own cycle. Direct links to removed or superseded records explain what happened. Render is read-only. Normalize malformed, repeated and impossible date parameters to a safe route; enforce a reasonable navigation horizon.
2. **Make task placement atomic.** Reuse the existing per-student lock, revision checks and receipt patterns. Add, move, resize, split/Undo and proposal confirmation must recheck the owning cycle, bounds, availability and occupants within the committing transaction. Write the task and its history together. Enforce the same verified/admitted/onboarded access in legacy diagnostic-start calls that Calendar still links to; source review found its start route authenticates without the shared application gate. A normal move may not change cycle ownership. Use the existing weekly continuation flow for cross-cycle work, or an explicit transfer preview with both cycles and revisions. Do not silently reassign historical rows. Audit existing off-cycle rows read-only; offer a repair from their recorded provenance instead of inferring from titles.
3. **Use one slot model.** Normalize protected classes, shifted/skipped occurrences, timed mocks/rehearsals, assessment appointments, other timed work and availability into the same local-day interval checks. Keep time already elapsed, sitting limits, rest days, full duration and midnight handling explicit. Reuse 20261127 checks; where the SQL currently relies on application-calculated limits, check authoritative policy again under the lock. A mock with only a date is not a timed reservation. Where a student keeps both a timed mock and the weekly paper, check the paper’s entire interval as well: the generator currently defaults the paper to a fixed closing-day clock time. Prefer a safe alternative or unplaced work when no interval fits. A day-preferred sitting can stay flexible, but the planner should prove at least one contiguous slot is feasible (or explicitly split it); three 15-minute gaps are not one 45-minute sitting. If manual availability overrides are retained, label them and require confirmation; protected appointments cannot be overridden.
4. **Guard the exam horizon and accepted history.** Keep the booked exam day free of new study and refuse new plans after the target. For a passed *indicative* date, ask whether to update the target or record having sat; do not restart first pass or infer the exam was taken. Represent the final partial week explicitly, trim tasks to the last allowed study day and move closure to the supported checkpoint. Preserve past results and recorded activity.

**Exit tests:** Past/current/future and malformed links; historical and foreign IDs; accepted-cycle revision changes; mock versus task in both write orders; exact adjacent slots; timed and untimed adjustments; overlap in concurrent requests; closing versus editing; IST midnight and a DST region; after-exam, postponed-exam and final partial week.

## Phase 2 — useful entry and later weeks

1. **Continue from the student's actual position.** Keep quick topic declarations and reading refinements already in onboarding. Show “covered once”, “partly covered”, “not yet covered” and “reported unsure” as distinct inputs; allow recent, older or unknown where supported. Build first-pass work from remaining portions only, preserving prerequisites. For the partly covered reading, show the next unfinished outcome, not a full reading restart. Use targeted revision on reported uncertainty and small maintenance elsewhere; scored weaknesses take priority when available. Make hours, count of distinct readings, and the capacity/evidence reason visible before acceptance. A short-horizon or low-capacity student sees trade-offs and work left waiting, never an impossible catch-up target.
2. **Keep the roadmap, week and task explanations in sync.** The current fixture's milestone header named FSA while the generated first-pass blocks included Corporate Issuers and Equity. Show the roadmap target separately from the work actually selected and explain intentional overlap, repairs and revision. Review whether selection should be restricted to the active milestone with a transparent next-milestone fallback. Do not drop unfinished material at a phase transition; keep it visible as deferred scope with a choice. Adapt budget using actual reported pace and closed-cycle outcomes, not a silently increased daily quota.
3. **Rollover from a canonical boundary.** Preserve the accepted cycle. For the successor use dated recurring occurrences and a confirmed variable next class, including starts/ends, cancellations and reschedules. Use the founder's class rule without discarding intervening study days: propose an explicit short transition or show a deliberate break if the calendar requires one. Self-study remains Mon–Sat learning, Sunday checkpoint. When an old cycle is closed after an absence, show the gap and propose one sensible recovery cycle.
4. **Keep revision a recurring practice.** An initial first pass uses a small revision allowance. A completed first pass goes into consolidation/revision, with recency based on actually recorded/confirmed activity and separate scored evidence. Rotate across readings and topics, prioritize overdue/uncertain/unstable outcomes, avoid repeats when uncovered rotation remains. Continue unfinished portions without counting a split reading twice. In the final stretch, protect rehearsal, debrief, evidence-backed repair and recovery. Keep unplaced re-checks visible with their dependency; never generate a clickable re-check with no eligible concept or bank. Audit `generateWeek.ts` repair/unstable lane suppression: its single per-week flag currently stops trying the lane after *any* item fits. Track which items were placed and which still wait.

5. **Offer a rest-of-week adjustment for an already populated plan.** The current-week proposal path handles empty accepted weeks only. Preview what happens after a missed day, a new class or less availability. Hold completed/started work and fixed commitments in place; propose only safe moves, a smaller remaining plan or work to carry forward. Show before/after minutes and exact moved, unchanged and deferred tasks, with a source version and one acceptance receipt. Do not duplicate work with another active plan.
6. **Make the next few weeks legible.** Show a rolling 2–4 week forecast with remaining topic targets, planned revision windows, mock dates and known trade-offs. Forecasts reuse the same solver and current constraints, remain read-only, and say which assumptions can move the dates. Never create future tasks, project scored mastery or lock a timetable before a student accepts it. Recompute from actual work and outcomes at each closure.

**Exit tests:** Fresh student; 3 topics covered with one partly covered reading; broad vs narrow uncertainty; first pass complete with recent/unknown revision; low capacity; one-off/fixed/ended classes; 4–6 real closures crossing phases; late return; source edit after preview; no-bank student; optional work never counted as missed; re-checks only after the required repair and spacing.

**Example of the intended midway journey:** A student reports three topics covered once and one reading half done, with a mock in four weeks. The first preview proposes the unfinished reading portion, new work from remaining topics and a small review of what feels uncertain. The next-weeks view shows likely focus and the mock date, with the forecast labelled. Weekly review reshapes that forecast from what was actually done. In the mock week, the student sees preparation, the reserved mock, and debrief time before accepting. After the mock, reported external results guide choices; only a scored Roadmap assessment creates diagnostic evidence and targeted repair. In the final stretch, the remaining syllabus and the work deliberately set aside both stay visible.

## Phase 3 — mocks from booking through review

- Show every owned mock on its actual week. Put timed mocks in the time grid and agenda, date-only mocks in the day lane. Keep a compact next-mock row above the plan and a full schedule behind a link. Home, Tests, Calendar, Coach and Settings must open the same appointment and assessment week. A future period is a forecast and an appointment editor, not an accepted study week.
- Pick the nearest **relevant upcoming** mock for each planned week. Both generator and revision selection currently start from `checkpoints[0]`; do not let an unresolved past appointment hide a later one. If two appointments share a week, let the student designate one, keep a weekly paper as well, or decide later. Key choices by appointment and canonical cycle so class edits don't transfer or orphan a decision. Audit whether the planner's current test of “any mock in this week” matches the designated ID.
- Make preparation actionable before the appointment. Show suggested revision slots, the mock reservation, debrief time and their impact in the proposed week. Today suggestions outside the open week are read-only; retain them as a forecast and make them selectable when that week is proposed. Merge by unique appointment + suggestion identity; keep existing study and fixed blocks visible. On a move/cancel, review related tasks and stale drafts before writing; never cancel a started assessment or silently shift accepted learning.
- External mock reporting is optional, attributed to its actual date, and remains self-reported. A postponed/not-taken outcome does not close a week by itself. A taken and correctly designated mock can. Internal rehearsal requires eligible reviewed held-out items, two timed sessions and an actual scored result. If the bank cannot serve it, show external/mock-prep options and keep planning usable; don't make a false promise. When the result creates repair work, use authoritative receipt task IDs for exact Calendar links, then re-check, then use the same weekly closure path.
- Harden answer save/resume as part of mock readiness. Retain unsaved input across timeout, check the server receipt before marking an answer saved, and reconcile pending saves before manual submission. Never extend the clock or score an answer the server did not receive before cutoff. At expiry distinguish genuinely skipped questions from visible unresolved save failures; preserve omission/evidence rules. Run ordinary sequential taking separately from a deliberate concurrency stress test; inspect lock timing and report every non-success answer rather than assuming a batch saved.
- Add a small “why these blocks” section with dates, recency, reported weaknesses, actual evidence and availability. First pass and mock prep can coexist; the mock does not declare the remaining syllabus complete or move the exam date.

**Exit tests:** Two mocks same week and a third later; choose one without affecting another; date-only vs timed; overlapping and out-of-horizon appointments; preparation before and debrief after; moving/cancelling after acceptance; late outcome; unavailable real bank; approved fixture two-session rehearsal → score → repair → re-check → closure. Capture actual browser clicks for the scored path before release, separate from HTTP coverage.

## Phase 4 — Coach correctness and planning assurance

- Make the server choose actions and wording from the same typed snapshot Calendar, Home and Tests use: actual paper capability and reason, selected week and exam status, available slots, blocked dependencies, booked versus date-only mock, self-reported versus scored evidence. The fallback must say why an assessment is unavailable and offer a usable next step (confirm scope, revise, log, external mock or review). Remove generic “take this week's paper” when it is not startable. Explain today deferral honestly: “set aside” does not mean “rescheduled”.
- Keep model authority limited to explanation. If an exact date, duration, topic, capacity figure, or actionable statement is needed, render it from server fields or require a reference to a server-issued fact. A JSON shape and regex policy are insufficient factual checks. Prefer deterministic UI copy for critical decisions. Open-question text must add no new schedule facts, commands, grading or tutoring. Use the same draft, revision, confirmations and permission checks for a Coach link as for Calendar.
- Diagnose the configured provider with bounded synthetic probes. Record whether it returned usable text, timeout, rejection, no content or invalid shape; verify fallback with an unavailable provider. The audit’s synthetic provider calls returned reasoning with empty content and finish reason `length` at 500 tokens; at 1,400, a separate call returned text that passed the JSON/reply validator. That is evidence to tune the response configuration, not a reason to increase every request budget blindly. Configure/test a supported response shape and an actual non-fallback smoke path before marking live wording healthy. Review history fields sent to the provider and keep inputs minimal. Model telemetry must omit tokens, student text and sensitive records by default.
- Add a seeded replay suite for the solver: one immutable snapshot plus clock and version yields the same work and reasons; enforce day and week capacity, cycle membership, no overlaps, ordering, stability, no duplicated readings or plans, and no writes until acceptance. Track uncovered time/reading estimates, reasons work waited, cross-surface disagreements, fallback rates and latency. Regression tests must cover empty, stale, partially complete, out-of-date, contradictory and adversarial facts, not just fluent replies.

**Exit tests:** Correct structured and fallback answers for no bank, next mock, revise, “what should I do today?”, exam sat, insufficient information, hostile input and fabricated exact times. Compare server plan with provider on/off; assert no task/time/score differences. Confirm one proposal once after seeing exact impact; a stale or foreign proposal must change nothing.

## Phase 5 — usable Calendar and release checks

- Make Today the primary action, with Week/Agenda and a clear date jump. Distinguish “checkpoint day” from “paper available”; an unavailable or pending paper must not look startable in the grid. Compress future mock information and tuck away history/secondary details. Group sittings by reading, with count, time and useful scope on expand. Clearly distinguish planned, done/partial, deferred, missed, unplaced and waiting for evidence. Support keyboard selection, focus return, undo, usable mobile controls, and errors without lost edits. Offer calm workload warnings and choices; never penalize low capacity as “not ready”.
- Measure Calendar reads and route time with a realistic history, multiple mocks and 2+ weeks. In the page today, independent schedule/profile/assessment reads are largely sequential; repeated lookups happen per day and appointment. Share only request-scoped owned reads with the same date/timezone/snapshot keys and move secondary data off the critical render. Add a calendar read contract and fail honestly if a core read fails. Do not cache mutable plans across users or serve a snapshot across a mutation. Some requests took more than two minutes during the concurrent development run; measure isolated baseline and stress timings separately before attributing this to query structure or lock contention. Return a retryable 503 when the admission database is unavailable while preserving the closed access boundary.
- Build automated student journeys with independently resettable fixtures. Split tests into actual-bank “cannot start, still plan” and fixture-bank “full assessment” cases. The Calendar suite currently expects five reviewed items and fails against the real bank; do not weaken eligibility. Make assertion diagnostics tolerate absent JSON fields, so a failed API read does not crash the rest of a suite. Replace obsolete checkpoint overload tests and verify no stale, incorrect links or false success messages. Use separate test banks or a test-run scope so concurrent suites cannot change eligibility mid-run. Assert fixture cleanup by recorded IDs. Run `npm run verify`, focused HTTP/transaction tests, isolated build and desktop + 390px browser journeys. Capture actual counts, skips, route/overlay/console checks and visual proof. Do not call Calendar release-ready until Phase 1 integrity and the applicable end-to-end gates are green.
